The game

Yie Ar Kung-Fu is a one-on-one fighting game: LEE against a line of opponents, each with his own weapon and his own way of moving. Everything that follows comes from reading the cartridge, not from playing it.

Their names are at 0x6C35, four tiles each, right-aligned with blank tiles:

Each brings his own pattern script and his own colour script, and that is the whole of the difference between one and another on screen: the temple in the background is the same for all five.

What the player can do

The state machine at 0x4D24 takes whatever movement the controls bring and dispatches it through a thirty-two entry table. The movements the code tells apart:

  • Walking, two pixels a frame, with the position in 0xE161 between 0x23 and 0xE0, and two poses alternating every eight frames.
  • Jumping, with the curve at 0x4DE1 adding into the height: six frames of −4, four of −3, two of −2, one of −1 and then the positives. Up fast, a pause at the top, and down.
  • Three standing strikes, each with its pose and its number. The number indexes the points table at 0x4B23: one hundred for the first and three hundred for the second and third.
  • The sweep, which is strike 4.
  • The mid-air strike, number 5: you have to let go of the button and press it again past frame 0x0B of the jump. 0x4E69 checks it, with three chained conditions.

The bonus stage

With 0xE058 set there is no opponent: the frame goes off to 0x79B7, which releases objects through the three slots at 0xE250 and counts the ones knocked down in 0xE275, the figure drawn beside the NUMBER OF HITS at 0x7BBB.

Which object comes out is decided by the table at 0x7A7C, read nibble by nibble: the two low bits pick the slot and the pattern, bit 2 the height and the sign of the speed, and bit 3 between two speeds. The X is kept in two bytes and the speed added with add and adc, sixteen bits: the lower byte is the fractional part, so an object can travel slower than one pixel a frame without the step showing.

How the opponent decides

He does not move at random, not entirely. 0x5476 measures the gap to the player and 0x5488 compares it, first against the three bytes the table at 0x54B8 gives for that opponent and then against three fixed caps — 0x40, 0x48 and 0x68 —, that is seven bands. The band picks the list, and the movement comes out of the list.

What randomness there is comes from 0x6D2C: a ten-byte shift register with a fixed seed, returning a number from 0 to 3. Each opponent has five character routines that share those four values out among four different movements.
