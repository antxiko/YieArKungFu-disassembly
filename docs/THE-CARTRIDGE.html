The cartridge

16,384 bytes in page 1, from 0x4000 to 0x7FFF. The BIOS reads the AB header and calls INIT.

The header

The first ten bytes are 41 42 10 40 00 00 00 00 00 00: it declares only INIT, and here INIT sits right against the header, at 0x4010. No STATEMENT, no DEVICE, no TEXT.

The VRAM map

The eight values at 0x4503, R0 to R7, are 02 E2 0E 7F 07 76 03 E0, and the whole map comes out of them:

wherefrom which register
COLOUR0x0000R3 = 0x7F
SPRITE PATTERNS0x1800R6 = 0x03
PATTERNS0x2000R4 = 0x07
NAMES0x3800R2 = 0x0E
SPRITE ATTRIBUTES0x3B00R5 = 0x76

R3 and R4 are not addresses: they are a base bit and a mask. What they say is colour below and pattern above, the reverse of the usual layout. And R1 = 0xE2, with bit 1 set and bit 0 clear, means 16x16 sprites, unmagnified.

These eight values are the same as two other cartridges of the same family except for R7, the border colour.

The two drawing interpreters

The cartridge does not store VRAM dumps: it stores short programs that get interpreted. There are two, and it pays not to mix them up.

SCRIPTS (the interpreter at 0x442C). They write through the VDP's data port, byte by byte, with two commands:

0x00           end
0x80           chain: the next two bytes are a new VRAM address,
               LOW BYTE FIRST
n (bit 7 clear)  fill: the next byte, repeated n times
n (bit 7 set)    raw copy of (n and 0x7F) bytes

TILE LISTS (the one at 0x44A6). They write into the name table with WRTVRM, one tile per byte, passing each through a mask that decides whether it is drawn or erased:

0xFF   end
0xFE   chain, the same as the other one's 0x80
rest   the byte is a tile

And every list begins with its address, because 0x449E — the normal door — only sets the mask and falls into 0x44A0, which is the body of command 0xFE: the first thing it does is read two bytes. The only entry that skips that is 0x449A.

The alphabet

It is not ASCII. A is tile 0x21 and on from there to Z at 0x3A; the digits run 0x10 to 0x19, the space is 0x20 — and 0x00, the blank tile, does the same job — and the copyright circle is 0x1A.

No need to assume it: KONAMI, PERFECT and GAME OVER read out on their own with that arrangement and no other alignment gives them.

The fifteen dispatch tables

The cartridge dispatches two ways. The first, 0x4071, has the table written behind the call itself: pop hl takes it off the stack, A doubled indexes into it, and whatever pointer comes out is executed with jp (hl). Since the return address has been popped, that does not come back.

The second, 0x7375, indexes a table of tables by the opponent and dispatches whichever comes out.

Without declaring all fifteen the static trace falls well short: the six of the per-opponent dispatch alone hide 315 bytes of code.

At the highest level the hard cartridge's opponent reacts four times as often as the easy one's, and on the easy cartridge that never happens at any level. Everything else is five constants and one change of form.

Byte for byte the two differ in 15,618 of 16,384, 95.3%. That figure means nothing — both are relocated. By instruction they share 97.0%. The detail of every difference is in Findings, and tools/coteja_builds.py produces it.
